Privacy Policy. Short, because Toll collects little.

Toll locks the apps you choose until you prove your habit with the camera. This page says what Toll does with your data: anonymous usage counts, a daily check mark for the friends you add, and never anything that says who you are.

No sign-up

Toll has no sign-up and no login. We cannot see who you are. Your habits, what you write and your photos never reach us; Toll sends only the anonymous counts described under Analytics and, if you add friends, the daily check mark described under Friends.

Photos

Photos come from Toll's camera only. They are checked on your iPhone by Apple's on-device image analysis and on-device language model, then discarded. Toll never stores a photo, never uploads a photo, and never reads your photo library.

What Toll keeps

Your habits (name, schedule, photos a day, kind, for your own habit the description you wrote, and a place or step goal if you give it one: the place’s name, address, spot on the map and size), proof records (the time of each proof, whether it was accepted and which check accepted it, never the photo), rest days, focus sessions (their times, and whether Strict mode held them), schedules and time limits (their names, days, times and minutes) live on your iPhone and in your private iCloud database, through your own iCloud account, so they follow you to a new iPhone. With iCloud off, they stay on this iPhone.

On this iPhone only, and in its backups: your name, your first-run answers (the kinds of habit you picked, your estimate of your daily phone time and the time of day you chose for reminders), your age and your reason for starting if you add them in Your details (setups before 1.4 asked for both), your settings (the block screen's words and your website switches), which time limits were reached today, the apps you chose to lock, and the last time you arrived at and left each habit’s place. If you set a PIN lock, only a salted fingerprint of the PIN is kept.

We have no access to any of it.

Screen Time

Toll uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks to lock the apps you pick. Apple does not tell Toll which apps those are; Toll works with tokens that stay on your iPhone. The Screen Time figures Toll shows (on Today, on the Screen Time page and on the score page) are drawn by iOS inside a Screen Time report that Toll cannot read, so the numbers never reach Toll and never leave your iPhone. To hold your time against the week before you started Toll, that report also reads when you started Toll and the phone time you guessed in setup, both kept on this iPhone. A time limit works the same way: iOS tells Toll only that the minutes you chose were used up, never which app or for how long.

Website protection turns on Apple's own adult-content filter and, while your apps are locked, blocks every website; Toll never learns which sites you visit. iOS turns off Safari's Private Browsing while a filter is on.

Toll never hides apps and never touches your passcode. If you turn on Strict mode, iOS also blocks deleting apps and keeps the date and time automatic while your apps are locked; both clear when they unlock. Deleting Toll removes every lock.

Location and Apple Health

Both are optional and asked for only when you give a habit a place or a step goal.

A place: Toll asks iOS to tell it when you arrive at and leave the place you picked (with Location set to Always, this works while Toll is closed). Toll uses it only to check you in and out of that habit. It never follows your route or notes where else you go, and the times you arrive and leave stay on this iPhone and are never sent. Searching for a place, or using where you are now, goes through Apple Maps, under Apple’s privacy policy.

Steps: Toll reads your step count from Apple Health on your iPhone to see whether a walk or run habit’s goal is reached. It never writes to Health, keeps no step history and never sends your steps anywhere; the habit’s proof record says only that the goal was reached. Steps typed into Health by hand don’t count. Toll never uses Health data for advertising.

Remove a place or a step goal in the habit’s editor, or turn off Toll’s Location or Health access in the Settings app, at any time.

Friends

Friends are optional. Nothing about friends leaves your iPhone until you make or accept an invite. Then Toll’s friends server keeps: a random ID made for this iPhone and a fingerprint of its secret key (no name, email or phone number); your time zone and language; your daily check mark (whether you proved today, took a rest day or had nothing due), your streak, your days in the month’s challenge and how many of this week’s due days you kept; when your Toll last reached the server; your invite codes; who you are paired with and whose nudges you muted; the nudges and cheers sent between you; and, if notifications are allowed for Toll, this iPhone’s push token. It never receives your name, photos, habits, apps, places, steps or Screen Time.

Only the friends you pair with, 3 at most, see your check mark, streak, challenge days and this week’s days, under the name they gave you on their own iPhone. The names you give friends stay on this iPhone. If you put your first name in an invite, it travels only in the link: the invite page shows it to whoever opens the link, and the server never keeps it. Invite pages have no cookies, no analytics and no scripts.

Nudges and cheers are fixed sentences: nobody can write to you, and no photos are shared. Their notifications are optional: turn them off with From friends on Toll’s Notifications page, and the Friends screen still shows everything. If notifications are allowed for Toll, they arrive only between 9 AM and 9 PM your time, 2 a day at most. On a friend’s card you can mute their nudges or remove them; removing deletes the pair and everything sent between you, and the friend isn’t told.

Cloudflare runs the server for us. Like every server, it sees your IP address with each request; Toll’s friends server keeps no request logs and never stores it (when a new ID is made, a scrambled form of the network address is counted for one minute to stop floods). Nudges and cheers are deleted after 30 days, invites once they expire 7 days after they are made, and everything about an iPhone whose Toll hasn’t reached the server for 90 days. Delete my friends data, in Friends or in Settings, deletes all of it at once, and this iPhone forgets its ID and the names it gave. Deleting Toll alone leaves it on the server until those 90 days have passed. See cloudflare.com/privacypolicy.

Purchases

Toll Pro is sold through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment; Toll never sees your payment details. RevenueCat, the service that checks purchases for Toll, receives an anonymous identifier its software creates on your iPhone and your Toll purchase and trial history, and with each request technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the App Store country, your preferred languages and an identifier Apple gives Toll on this iPhone, plus your IP address, which every server sees. This happens when Toll loads the plans or checks Toll Pro, from the first launch. When the plans page is shown, RevenueCat also records that it was viewed, to count how many people see the plans; it carries the same anonymous identifier and nothing about who you are. RevenueCat never receives your name, email, photos or habits. See revenuecat.com/privacy.

Notifications

Toll sends only the notifications listed on its Notifications page, each with its own switch, and only with your permission: the evening reminder on days a habit is still due, a note an hour before a due habit's day ends, a morning plan, a note before a streak ends, a weekly recap, a note before a schedule locks your apps or when a time limit is reached, a note when a focus session ends, a note when you have stayed long enough at a habit’s place, and, after you start a free week, one reminder 2 days before it ends, in time to cancel. Streak at risk, the weekly recap and the place note start on once you allow notifications, and the free week's reminder when you ask for it; the others are off until you turn them on. Each can be turned off there or in the Settings app. They are planned on your iPhone. The one exception is Friends: friends’ nudges and cheers come from Toll’s friends server (see Friends); turn them off with the From friends switch, which shows once you use Friends, mute a friend’s nudges on their card, or turn off Toll’s notifications in the Settings app.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Toll sends anonymous usage counts to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service built for privacy, so we can see where Toll is hard to use: when Toll is opened; which first-run pages are reached and when the first run is finished; when the practice photo on the demo page is taken (with the habit's kind; it is a drawing, never a real photo); the range you pick for your daily phone time (such as "4 to 6 hours"); whether you ask for the free week's reminder and what iOS answered; each step of the Screen Time page (shown, continue, approved, declined, try again, and the type of error if one happens); how many apps you chose to lock, as a range (none, 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 or more), never which ones; how many days your first habit is due and the time of day you chose for its reminder; when the plans are shown and which plan you tap; when a purchase starts, completes, fails or is cancelled (with the plan and whether it has a free week), and when the renewal is turned off during the free week; when a proof is accepted (a photo, a stay at a habit's place or a step goal) or a photo is turned down (with the habit's kind and which check decided, and for an accepted proof, whether it came on the day you set up Toll); when a habit, schedule or time limit is made (with its kind); when a challenge's page is opened (with which challenge); and in Friends: when Friends is opened (and from where: the Friends tab, Today, a notification or a link), when an invite is made and shared (and whether it carries a first name, never the name), when you accept an invite (by link or by code), how many friends you have after a pairing (1, 2 or 3), when a nudge or a cheer is sent (with which cheer), when a friend's nudges are muted or unmuted, when a friend is removed, when your friends data is deleted, and your answer to the notification question (allowed, not allowed or later). Each count carries an anonymous identifier (a scrambled form of the one iOS gives Toll on this iPhone) and technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, and settings such as language, region, time zone and text size. A count never carries your name, what you write, your photos, your apps, the websites you visit, where you are, your steps or your friends. TelemetryDeck does not store IP addresses. See telemetrydeck.com/privacy.

Toll shows no ads, does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not sell any data. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app, Apple may send us crash reports; they contain no photos and do not say who you are.

Toll's ads on Instagram and Facebook

Toll advertises on Instagram and Facebook. To see whether those ads work, Toll includes Meta's app SDK: when Toll is installed and opened, and when a free week or a purchase starts, it tells Meta so, with technical details such as the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the language and the time zone, and an anonymous identifier Meta's software creates on your iPhone. Toll never asks to track you, so Meta gets no advertising identifier and iOS blocks Meta's tracking address; whether an ad led to an install reaches Meta through Apple's SKAdNetwork, which does not say who you are. Meta never receives your name, what you write, your photos, your habits, your apps or where you are. See facebook.com/privacy/policy.

Your data, your choices

Apart from what the friends server keeps, everything Toll keeps is on your devices and in your own iCloud, so you can see it, export it and delete it yourself. Export data in Toll's Settings writes your habits, proof records, rest days and focus sessions to a file you keep. Deleting Toll deletes its data from the iPhone; Toll's iCloud data is deleted from your iCloud storage in the Settings app. Delete my friends data, in Friends or in Settings, deletes what the friends server keeps. To have the purchase records RevenueCat holds deleted, write to us.

Children

Toll is for people managing their own phone. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Toll's website

tollapp.github.io has no cookies, no analytics and no scripts, and loads nothing from other services. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visits, including IP addresses, to keep the service secure, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version is published in the app and on tollapp.github.io/privacy.

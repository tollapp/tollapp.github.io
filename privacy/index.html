Privacy Policy. Short, because Toll collects little.

Toll locks the apps you choose until you prove your habit with the camera. This page says what Toll does with your data: usage counts that carry no name and, only if you use Friends, clans or Communities, what those need, such as your check mark, your name in Toll, and your messages, posts and comments. Toll never keeps your photos and never asks for your email address or phone number.

No sign-up

Toll has no sign-up and no login, and it asks for no email address or phone number. Your photos never reach us. Your habits stay on your iPhone and in your iCloud, except what you share through Friends, clans and Communities. Toll sends only the counts described under Analytics, which carry no name, and, if you use those, what is described under Friends, clans and communities.

Photos

Photos come from Toll's camera only. They are checked on your iPhone by Apple's on-device image analysis and on-device language model, then discarded. Toll never stores a photo, never uploads a photo, and never reads your photo library.

What Toll keeps

Your habits (name, schedule, photos a day, kind, for your own habit the description you wrote, and a place or step goal if you give it one: the place's name, address, spot on the map and size), proof records (the time and date of each proof, whether it was accepted, which check accepted it and which plan you were on, never the photo), rest days, focus sessions (their times, and whether Strict mode held them), schedules and time limits (their names, days, times and minutes), your best streak and the dates you earned badges live on your iPhone and in your private iCloud database, through your own iCloud account, so they follow you to a new iPhone. If you don't use iCloud, they stay on this iPhone. If you sign out of iCloud or turn iCloud off for Toll, iOS removes them from this iPhone; they stay in your iCloud and come back when you turn it on again.

On this iPhone only, and in its backups: your name, your first-run answers (the kinds of habit you picked, your estimate of your daily phone time and the time of day you chose for reminders), your reason for starting if you add it in Your details, your age range if you share it (under 13, 13 to 15, or 16 and over; never your age), your settings (the block screen's words and your website switches), which time limits were reached today, the apps you chose to lock, and the last time you arrived at and left each habit's place. If you set a PIN lock, only a salted fingerprint of the PIN is kept.

We have no access to any of it, apart from the counts under Analytics, which include the kind of your first habit, your phone-time range and your reminder's time of day.

Screen Time

Toll uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks to lock the apps you pick. Apple does not tell Toll which apps those are; Toll works with tokens that stay on your iPhone. The Screen Time figures Toll shows (on Today, on the Screen Time page and on the score page) are drawn by iOS inside a Screen Time report that Toll cannot read, so the numbers never reach Toll and never leave your iPhone. To hold your time against the week before you started Toll, that report also reads when you started Toll and the phone time you guessed in setup, both kept on this iPhone. A time limit works the same way: iOS tells Toll only that the minutes you chose were used up, never which app or for how long.

Website protection turns on Apple's own adult-content filter and, while your apps are locked, blocks every website; Toll never learns which sites you visit. iOS turns off Safari's Private Browsing while a filter is on.

Toll never hides apps and never touches your passcode. If you turn on Strict mode, iOS also blocks deleting apps and keeps the date and time automatic while your apps are locked; both clear when they unlock. Deleting Toll removes every lock.

Location and Apple Health

Both are optional and asked for only when you give a habit a place or a step goal.

A place: Toll asks iOS to tell it when you arrive at and leave the place you picked (with Location set to Always, this works while Toll is closed). Toll uses it only to check you in and out of that habit. It never follows your route or notes where else you go, and the times you arrive and leave stay on this iPhone. When a stay proves a habit, the proof's time goes where any proof's time goes: to friends who see your habits, your clans and your communities, unless you hide the habit. Searching for a place, or using where you are now, goes through Apple Maps, under Apple's privacy policy.

Steps: Toll reads your step count from Apple Health on your iPhone to see whether a walk or run habit's goal is reached. It never writes to Health, keeps no step history and never sends your steps anywhere; the habit's proof record says only that the goal was reached. Steps typed into Health by hand don't count. Toll never uses Health data for advertising.

Remove a place or a step goal in the habit's editor, or turn off Toll's Location or Health access in the Settings app, at any time.

Friends, clans and communities

Friends, clans and communities are optional, and closed to anyone under 13 (see Children). Nothing about you leaves your iPhone for them until you make or accept an invite, enter a code, start or join a clan, or join a community; before that, Toll only reads the public list of communities, with no ID. Then Toll's server keeps: a random ID made for this iPhone and a fingerprint of its secret key (no email address or phone number); your time zone, language and Toll version; your age answer (16 or over, under 16, or not shared; never your age); your daily check mark (whether you proved today, took a rest day or had nothing due), your streak, your days in the month's challenge and how many of this week's due days you kept; when your Toll last reached the server; your invite codes; who you are paired with, your partner, and whose nudges you muted; the nudges and cheers sent between you; and, if notifications are allowed for Toll, this iPhone's push token.

If you share your habits, are in a clan or a community, or picked a partner, it also keeps, for each habit you have not hidden, the kind of habit (such as gym or reading), this week's days and the time of each proof; friends alone never get this list while you don't share your habits. The name you gave a habit is kept only while you share your habits: stop sharing, or hide the habit, and its name is cleared, from your clan cards too. If you start or join a clan or join a community, it also keeps the name you type (20 characters at most, checked against word lists), your color, and when you accepted the chat rules and the community rules. It also keeps your messages and reactions; your posts and comments in communities; your activity page (who reacted to or commented on your posts, the first 60 characters of a comment, and what came of your reports); which clans and communities you are in; your days on each weekly ranking; what you last read in each chat; the people you blocked and the reports you sent; and counts that limit how much you can post and how many of your reports were dismissed. It never receives your photos, apps, places, steps or Screen Time.

Posting, commenting and chatting in communities are for 16 and over, and nobody who said they are under 16 can have a partner or be one. Toll asks iOS for your age range (Apple's Declared Age Range) the first time you use Friends, clans or communities, and the server keeps only your answer: 16 or over, under 16, or not shared. An answer of under 16 holds for 30 days before you can check again. If you are under 16 or didn't share it, nobody else sees you in a community.

Who sees what. The friends you pair with, 20 at most, see your check mark, streak, challenge days and this week's days, under the name they gave you on their own iPhone. If you choose to share your habits, they also see the names of your habits and the time you proved each. You can hide any habit; from then on it is hidden from clans and communities too. The names you give friends stay on this iPhone. Only the people in a clan (20 at most, and you can be in 3) see your name in Toll, your messages and reactions, your place on the clan's weekly ranking, and a card when you prove a habit, which shows the habit's name only if you share your habits. Everyone in a community, and anyone can join, sees your name in Toll and color, your streak and whether you proved today, the kind of habit you proved (never its name) and when, your days this week, and your posts, comments and reactions. Any Toll user can see a community's weekly ranking. If you put your first name in an invite, it travels only in the link: the invite page shows it to whoever opens the link, and the server never keeps it. Invite pages have no cookies, no analytics and no scripts.

Chat and reports. The server checks every message, post and comment in a community, and every name in Toll, against word lists in the app's languages, and refuses links, email addresses, phone numbers and @handles. No AI reads anything. Messages between friends and in clans aren't checked against the word lists; links, email addresses, phone numbers and @handles are refused there too when someone in the chat is under 16 or didn't share an age range. You can report a message, a post, a comment, a card, a person or a name, and block a person; blocking removes the pair and hides that person from you. A report sends Toll's developer what was reported, the reason and any note, the name and ID of who wrote it, how long that ID has been on Toll, how many times it was reported before and whether it is banned, and the 10 items around it. We aim to look at every report within 24 hours, and then remove what was reported, ban the person or close the report; the person who reported it is told what came of it. In a community, a post or comment reported by 3 different people is hidden for everyone until a person at Toll has looked at it. Messages, posts and comments aren't end-to-end encrypted: Cloudflare encrypts them when stored and sent, and Toll's developer can reach them. He reads them when something is reported.

How long it is kept. Messages, posts and comments: 30 days. Your activity page: 30 days. Proof cards and notes such as "started the clan": 14 days. Reactions go with what they were on. Your habit list is replaced each time Toll sends it, its names are cleared as soon as you stop sharing, and the list is cleared after 14 days without an update. A clan stays until the person who started it deletes it or the last member leaves, and its invites are deleted 7 days after they are made. A community's rankings are kept for this week and the two weeks before. Nudges and cheers: 30 days. Friend invites: until they expire, 7 days after they are made. Your partner's asks: 14 days. A report: until it is handled, then 90 days with a copy of what was reported (the 10 items around it go once it is handled); a report nobody handled goes after 90 days. A report that a child may be at risk keeps the words it is about, with the name and ID that wrote them and the reporter's note; it has no set end date. A ban: until it ends. Your name, age answer and rules times: while your ID exists. Everything about an iPhone whose Toll hasn't reached the server for 90 days is deleted.

Cloudflare runs the server for us. Like every server, it sees your IP address with each request; Toll's server keeps no request logs and never stores it (when a new ID is made, a scrambled form of the network address is counted for one minute to stop floods). Cloudflare handles network records under its own policy, which sets no fixed time. Deleted data can stay in Cloudflare's restore history for up to 30 days, used only to recover from a failure; then it is gone. Toll uses the country of your connection and your App Store country to apply local age rules (see Children), and doesn't keep them. See cloudflare.com/privacypolicy.

Delete my friends and communities data, in Friends, in Communities or in Settings, deletes all of it at once: your friends and the chats between you, your partner and your asks, your messages, posts, comments, cards and reactions in every clan and community, your memberships, blocks and bans, your name and your check marks. A clan you started passes to its longest member, or is deleted if you were its only member. A report you made, or that names you, keeps a copy of what was reported for 90 days after it is handled, no longer linked to your ID; a report that a child may be at risk keeps the words it is about, as above. This iPhone forgets its ID and the names it gave. Deleting Toll alone leaves the data on the server until it has gone 90 days without use. Removing a friend deletes the pair and the chat between you, and the friend isn't told.

Your streak with a friend

To count the days you and a friend both proved, Toll's server keeps your daily check mark for your last 400 days: whether you proved, missed, took a rest day or had nothing due. Your friend sees only how many days you both proved and which of you still has to prove today. It is deleted with the rest of your friends data.

Story cards

A story card is a picture your iPhone draws from your own numbers. It stays on your iPhone until you send it yourself, and Toll never receives it. Toll counts when the cards are opened and when Share is tapped, with which card (proof, streak or week), in the same way as the counts under Analytics.

Your partner

If you choose a partner, Toll's server keeps who you picked until either of you ends it. Your partner sees your status, each habit's kind and when you proved it, and its name only if you share habits with friends; a habit you hide stays hidden. They see when you ask for a rest day and answer yes or no. An ask holds a day and an answer, never a reason or any words, and is kept for 14 days. If your partner says yes, your iPhone rests that day. A partner can never lock your apps. Nobody who said they are under 16 can have a partner or be one. Deleting your friends data removes your partner and your asks; an answer you gave someone stays with their ask, without your ID. Toll counts, without names, when a partner is chosen or removed and when an ask is sent, answered or applied.

Purchases

Toll Pro is sold through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment; Toll never sees your payment details. RevenueCat, the service that checks purchases for Toll, receives an identifier its software creates on your iPhone, which doesn't show your name, and your Toll purchase and trial history, and with each request technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the App Store country, your preferred languages and an identifier Apple gives Toll on this iPhone, plus your IP address, which every server sees. This happens when Toll loads the plans or checks Toll Pro, from the first launch. When the plans page is shown, RevenueCat also records that it was viewed, to count how many people see the plans; it carries the same identifier and nothing about who you are. RevenueCat never receives your name, email, photos or habits. See revenuecat.com/privacy.

Toll's ads on the App Store

To see whether Toll's ads in the App Store (Apple Ads) work, Toll asks iOS once, after it is installed, for a token from Apple's AdServices framework and passes it to RevenueCat, which sends it to Apple. Apple answers only whether Toll was installed from an Apple Ads ad and, if so, which one: its campaign, ad group, search keyword and country. RevenueCat keeps that answer with its identifier for this iPhone. The token is not an advertising identifier, Toll never asks to track you, and the answer never says who you are. See Apple's privacy notice for Apple Ads.

Notifications

Toll sends only the notifications listed on its Notifications page, each with its own switch, and only with your permission: the evening reminder on days a habit is still due, a note an hour before a due habit's day ends, a morning plan, a note before a streak ends, a weekly recap, a note before a schedule locks your apps or when a time limit is reached, a note when a focus session ends, a note when you have stayed long enough at a habit's place, and, after you start a free week, one reminder 2 days before it ends, in time to cancel. Streak at risk, the weekly recap and the place note start on once you allow notifications, and the free week's reminder when you ask for it; the others are off until you turn them on. Each can be turned off there or in the Settings app. They are planned on your iPhone. The exception is Friends, clans and communities: nudges and cheers from friends, new messages from a friend or in a clan, new proofs and new members in a clan, a comment on your post or a reply to your comment in a community, last week's result on Monday morning, and a partner's ask for a rest day and its answer come from Toll's server (see Friends, clans and communities). No notification holds the words of a message, post or comment; your iPhone writes each sentence from names it already has. A new post in a community never sends a notification, and comments and replies send at most one for a post every 30 minutes. They arrive only between 9 AM and 9 PM your time (8 AM to 10 PM for a friend's message or a partner's ask), 3 a day at most from friends, clans and communities together, not counting a partner's ask and its answer, and a chat sends at most one every 10 minutes (30 for a clan); nudges and cheers stay at 2 a day. Turn them off with the Friends and communities switch, which shows once you use them, mute a friend's nudges or a chat's messages, or turn off Toll's notifications in the Settings app.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Toll sends usage counts to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service built for privacy, so we can see where Toll is hard to use: when Toll is opened; which first-run pages are reached, the first one included, and when the first run is finished (with whether you are on Toll Pro or the free plan); when the practice photo on the demo page is taken (with the habit's kind; it is a drawing, never a real photo); the range you pick for your daily phone time (such as "4 to 6 hours"); whether you ask for the free week's reminder and what iOS answered; each step of the Screen Time page (shown, continue, approved, declined, try again, and the type of error if one happens, with whether you are on Toll Pro or the free plan); whether you chose apps to lock or skipped that page, never which apps or how many; how many days your first habit is due and the time of day you chose for its reminder; when the plans are shown and which plan you tap (with what opened them, such as more habits, more days, schedules, Strict mode or past scores); when a purchase starts, completes, fails or is cancelled (with the plan, where the plans were shown and what opened them, and whether it has a free week; for each tap, how it ended, whether Apple's purchase sheet came up, how long it stayed up and how long the tap waited for it, as ranges, whether Toll went to the background meanwhile, how many tries this is in this run of Toll, what the App Store said about a free week, the App Store's country, and the error's number if one happens), when Toll was closed with that sheet up, and when the renewal is turned off during the free week; when the free plan is chosen, when its second day of a week is proved, and when a session is started from its Block your apps now; how each focus session ended (it ran out or was ended early), with where it was started, its length as a range and whether Strict mode held it; when a proof is accepted (a photo, a stay at a habit's place or a step goal) or a photo is turned down (with the habit's kind, which check decided and whether it came on the day you set up Toll, and for an accepted proof, your plan and your day streak as a range: 0, 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 7, 8 to 14, 15 to 30 or more); when a habit, schedule or time limit is made (with its kind); when a challenge's page is opened (with which challenge); when the free plan's dates could not be saved on the iPhone (with the error's number); and in Friends, clans and communities: when Friends or Communities is opened (and from where: the tab, Today, a notification or a link); when an invite is made and shared (whether it carries a first name, never the name, and whether the share sheet sent it); when you accept an invite (by link or by code, and whether it made you someone's partner); how many friends you have after a pairing (1, 2, 3, 4 to 5, 6 to 10 or 11 to 20); when a nudge or a cheer is sent (with which cheer); when a friend's page is opened (from the list, the ranking or a clan); when a friend's nudges are muted or unmuted; when a friend is removed; whether you share your habits and where you answered (a question, the invite page or Settings); when a habit is hidden from friends or shown again; when a chat is opened and when a message is sent (with whether it is a chat with a friend, a clan or a community, never the message); when Toll refuses a message and the kind of reason, such as a banned word, a link, a contact detail or sending too fast; when someone reacts (with which of the four fixed reactions); when something is reported (a message, post, comment or card, a person, a name or a clan name) or a person is blocked, and where; when you set or change your name in Toll; which rules you accept; whether you shared your age range; when a clan is started, joined (by link or by code) or left (and whether you started it), when a clan's invite is shared (and whether the share sheet sent it) and its ranking is opened; when a community is opened (from the tab, a notification or a link), joined or left, or its ranking opened (with which community); when a post or comment is made in a community (with which community, never the words); when the Activity page is opened; when your friends and communities data is deleted; and your answer to the notification question (allowed, not allowed or later). Each count carries an identifier that doesn't show your name (a scrambled form of the one iOS gives Toll on this iPhone) and technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, and settings such as language, region, time zone, text size and accessibility settings. A count never carries your name, what you write, your photos, your apps, the websites you visit, where you are, your steps or your friends. TelemetryDeck does not store IP addresses. If iOS says you are under 13, Toll sends no counts at all. See telemetrydeck.com/docs/guides/privacy-faq.

Toll shows no ads, does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not sell any data. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app, Apple may send us crash reports; they contain no photos and do not say who you are.

Your data, your choices

Apart from what Toll's server keeps for friends, clans and communities, everything Toll keeps is on your devices and in your own iCloud, so you can see it, export it and delete it yourself. Export data in Toll's Settings writes your habits, proof records, rest days and focus sessions to a file you keep. Deleting Toll deletes its data from the iPhone, except what iOS usually keeps in its Keychain: the dates of your free plan's proved days, a record of the latest date Toll saw and the latest date a server gave it (so a date moved back can be told), and, if you used Friends, clans or Communities, this iPhone's random ID and its secret key. Installing Toll again on this iPhone usually reconnects that ID to your friends and clans, until our server deletes it. The proved days' dates are kept in the iPhone's Keychain, in iCloud Keychain if it is on, and in your iCloud, and they usually outlive deleting Toll, so installing Toll again usually does not reset the free plan's count for the week. They hold dates only. Toll's iCloud data is deleted from your iCloud storage in the Settings app. Delete my friends and communities data, in Friends, Communities or Settings, deletes what the server keeps, apart from what a report keeps. To have the purchase records RevenueCat holds deleted, write to us.

Who is responsible

Toll is provided by its developer, named on Toll's App Store page, who decides how your data is used and works from Lebanon. Cloudflare (Toll's server), RevenueCat (purchases) and TelemetryDeck (usage counts) process data for us under their own terms, and some of it can be outside your country.

Why we may use your data: to run what you ask for (your habits and locks, Toll Pro if you buy it, and your friends, clans, communities and posts); to keep people safe, which is our legitimate interest (word lists, reports, bans and your age range); and to see, through counts that carry no name, where Toll is hard to use, which is also our legitimate interest. You can object to the last two. Some of it the law requires, such as online safety rules.

Your rights

You can ask to see your data, correct it, delete it, move it, limit how we use it, or object. Most of it is on your iPhone and in your iCloud, where you can do this yourself (see Your data, your choices); for the rest, write to us. You can complain to the data protection authority in your country, or in the UK to the Information Commissioner.

Children

Toll is for people managing their own phone, and it is not directed at children under 13. The first time you use Friends, clans or communities, Toll asks iOS for your age range (Apple's Declared Age Range), with the ages 13 and 16. If it says you are under 13, nothing about Friends, clans or communities leaves your iPhone, any server ID is deleted, both tabs close and Toll sends no usage counts; everything else in Toll still works, and Check my age again, in Settings, asks again. From 13, Friends and clans are open. Posting, commenting and chatting in communities are for 16 and over; someone under 16, or who didn't share an age range, isn't shown to others there.

Some countries have their own rules. In Australia, Friends and Communities are for 16 and over, with the age range shared. In Brazil, and in Greece from 1 January 2027, communities are for 16 and over, with the age range shared. Communities aren't available in Indonesia, and Friends and Communities aren't available in Mississippi and Tennessee. Toll uses the country of your connection and your App Store country to apply these rules, and doesn't keep them.

Toll's website

tollapp.github.io has no cookies, no analytics and no scripts, and loads nothing from other services. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visits, including IP addresses, to keep the service secure, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version is published in the app and on tollapp.github.io/privacy.

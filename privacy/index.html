Privacy Policy. Short, because Toll collects little.

Toll locks the apps you choose until you prove your habit with the camera. This page says what Toll does with your data: anonymous usage counts and, only if you use Friends, clans or Communities, the check mark, name and messages those need. Toll never keeps your photos and never asks for your email address or phone number.

No sign-up

Toll has no sign-up and no login, and it asks for no email address or phone number. Your photos never reach us. Your habits stay on your iPhone and in your iCloud, except what you share through Friends, clans and Communities. Toll sends only the anonymous counts described under Analytics and, if you use those, what is described under Friends, clans and communities.

Photos

Photos come from Toll's camera only. They are checked on your iPhone by Apple's on-device image analysis and on-device language model, then discarded. Toll never stores a photo, never uploads a photo, and never reads your photo library.

What Toll keeps

Your habits (name, schedule, photos a day, kind, for your own habit the description you wrote, and a place or step goal if you give it one: the place’s name, address, spot on the map and size), proof records (the time of each proof, whether it was accepted and which check accepted it, never the photo), rest days, focus sessions (their times, and whether Strict mode held them), schedules and time limits (their names, days, times and minutes) live on your iPhone and in your private iCloud database, through your own iCloud account, so they follow you to a new iPhone. With iCloud off, they stay on this iPhone.

On this iPhone only, and in its backups: your name, your first-run answers (the kinds of habit you picked, your estimate of your daily phone time and the time of day you chose for reminders), your age and your reason for starting if you add them in Your details (setups before 1.4 asked for both), your settings (the block screen's words and your website switches), which time limits were reached today, the apps you chose to lock, and the last time you arrived at and left each habit’s place. If you set a PIN lock, only a salted fingerprint of the PIN is kept.

We have no access to any of it.

Screen Time

Toll uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks to lock the apps you pick. Apple does not tell Toll which apps those are; Toll works with tokens that stay on your iPhone. The Screen Time figures Toll shows (on Today, on the Screen Time page and on the score page) are drawn by iOS inside a Screen Time report that Toll cannot read, so the numbers never reach Toll and never leave your iPhone. To hold your time against the week before you started Toll, that report also reads when you started Toll and the phone time you guessed in setup, both kept on this iPhone. A time limit works the same way: iOS tells Toll only that the minutes you chose were used up, never which app or for how long.

Website protection turns on Apple's own adult-content filter and, while your apps are locked, blocks every website; Toll never learns which sites you visit. iOS turns off Safari's Private Browsing while a filter is on.

Toll never hides apps and never touches your passcode. If you turn on Strict mode, iOS also blocks deleting apps and keeps the date and time automatic while your apps are locked; both clear when they unlock. Deleting Toll removes every lock.

Location and Apple Health

Both are optional and asked for only when you give a habit a place or a step goal.

A place: Toll asks iOS to tell it when you arrive at and leave the place you picked (with Location set to Always, this works while Toll is closed). Toll uses it only to check you in and out of that habit. It never follows your route or notes where else you go, and the times you arrive and leave stay on this iPhone and are never sent. Searching for a place, or using where you are now, goes through Apple Maps, under Apple’s privacy policy.

Steps: Toll reads your step count from Apple Health on your iPhone to see whether a walk or run habit’s goal is reached. It never writes to Health, keeps no step history and never sends your steps anywhere; the habit’s proof record says only that the goal was reached. Steps typed into Health by hand don’t count. Toll never uses Health data for advertising.

Remove a place or a step goal in the habit’s editor, or turn off Toll’s Location or Health access in the Settings app, at any time.

Friends, clans and communities

Friends, clans and communities are optional. Nothing about them leaves your iPhone until you make or accept an invite, start or join a clan, or join a community. Then Toll’s server keeps: a random ID made for this iPhone and a fingerprint of its secret key (no email address or phone number); your time zone, language and Toll version; your daily check mark (whether you proved today, took a rest day or had nothing due), your streak, your days in the month’s challenge and how many of this week’s due days you kept; when your Toll last reached the server; your invite codes; who you are paired with and whose nudges you muted; the nudges and cheers sent between you; and, if notifications are allowed for Toll, this iPhone’s push token.

If you start or join a clan or join a community, it also keeps the name you type (20 characters at most, checked against word lists) and when you accepted the chat rules and the community rules. For each habit you have not hidden, it keeps the kind of habit (such as gym or reading), this week’s days and the time of each proof. The name you gave a habit is kept only while you share your habits. It also keeps your messages and reactions, which clans and communities you are in, your days on each weekly ranking, what you last read in each chat, the people you blocked and the reports you sent. It never receives your photos, apps, places, steps or Screen Time.

Chat in communities is for people 16 and over. Toll asks iOS for your age range (Apple’s Declared Age Range) and keeps only whether you are 16 or over, not your age. When you join a community, and at most once a week after that, Toll also sends Apple’s DeviceCheck token for this iPhone, so that a ban from Toll’s team can hold for the iPhone and not only for the random ID. The server keeps the token for 30 days, and Apple keeps the mark it sets.

Who sees what. The friends you pair with, 20 at most, see your check mark, streak, challenge days and this week’s days, under the name they gave you on their own iPhone. If you choose to share your habits, they also see the names of your habits and the time you proved each. You can hide any habit, and a hidden habit is hidden from clans and communities too. The names you give friends stay on this iPhone. Only the people in a clan (20 at most, and you can be in 3) see your name in Toll, your messages and reactions, your place on the clan’s weekly ranking, and a card when you prove a habit, which shows the habit’s name only if you share your habits. Everyone in a community, and anyone can join, sees your name in Toll, the kind of habit you proved (never its name) and when, your days this week, and your messages and reactions. If you put your first name in an invite, it travels only in the link: the invite page shows it to whoever opens the link, and the server never keeps it. Invite pages have no cookies, no analytics and no scripts.

Chat and reports. The server checks every message in a community, and every name in Toll, against word lists in the app’s languages, and refuses links, email addresses, phone numbers and @handles. No AI reads anything. Messages between friends and in clans are not checked first. You can report a message, a card, a person or a name, and block a person; blocking removes the pair and hides that person from you. A report sends Toll’s developer what was reported, the name of who wrote it and the 10 messages around it. He reads it and, within 24 hours, removes it, bans the person or closes the report. In a community, a message reported by 3 different people is hidden for everyone until he has looked at it.

How long it is kept. Messages: 30 days. Proof cards and notes such as "started the clan": 14 days. Reactions go with what they were on. Your habit list is replaced each time Toll sends it, its names are cleared as soon as you stop sharing, and the list is cleared after 14 days without an update. A clan stays until the person who started it deletes it or the last member leaves, and its invites are deleted 7 days after they are made. A community’s rankings are kept for this week and the two weeks before. Nudges and cheers: 30 days. Friend invites: until they expire, 7 days after they are made. A report, with what was reported and the 10 messages around it: 90 days after it is handled. A ban: until it ends. Your name, age answer and rules times: while your ID exists. Everything about an iPhone whose Toll hasn’t reached the server for 90 days is deleted.

Cloudflare runs the server for us. Like every server, it sees your IP address with each request; Toll’s server keeps no request logs and never stores it (when a new ID is made, a scrambled form of the network address is counted for one minute to stop floods). See cloudflare.com/privacypolicy.

Delete my friends and communities data, in Friends, in Communities or in Settings, deletes all of it at once: your friends and the chats between you, your messages, cards and reactions in every clan and community, your memberships, blocks and bans, your name and your check marks. A clan you started passes to its longest member, or is deleted if you were its only member. A report you made, or that names you, keeps a copy of what was reported for 90 days after it is handled, no longer linked to your ID. This iPhone forgets its ID and the names it gave. If you were banned, Apple’s DeviceCheck keeps its mark for this iPhone. Deleting Toll alone leaves the data on the server until it has gone 90 days without use. Removing a friend deletes the pair and the chat between you, and the friend isn’t told.

Purchases

Toll Pro is sold through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment; Toll never sees your payment details. RevenueCat, the service that checks purchases for Toll, receives an anonymous identifier its software creates on your iPhone and your Toll purchase and trial history, and with each request technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the App Store country, your preferred languages and an identifier Apple gives Toll on this iPhone, plus your IP address, which every server sees. This happens when Toll loads the plans or checks Toll Pro, from the first launch. When the plans page is shown, RevenueCat also records that it was viewed, to count how many people see the plans; it carries the same anonymous identifier and nothing about who you are. RevenueCat never receives your name, email, photos or habits. See revenuecat.com/privacy.

Notifications

Toll sends only the notifications listed on its Notifications page, each with its own switch, and only with your permission: the evening reminder on days a habit is still due, a note an hour before a due habit's day ends, a morning plan, a note before a streak ends, a weekly recap, a note before a schedule locks your apps or when a time limit is reached, a note when a focus session ends, a note when you have stayed long enough at a habit’s place, and, after you start a free week, one reminder 2 days before it ends, in time to cancel. Streak at risk, the weekly recap and the place note start on once you allow notifications, and the free week's reminder when you ask for it; the others are off until you turn them on. Each can be turned off there or in the Settings app. They are planned on your iPhone. The exception is Friends, clans and communities: nudges and cheers from friends, new messages from a friend or in a clan, new proofs and new members in a clan, and last week’s result on Monday morning come from Toll’s server (see Friends, clans and communities). No notification holds the words of a message; your iPhone writes each sentence from names it already has. A message in a community never sends a notification. They arrive only between 9 AM and 9 PM your time (8 AM to 10 PM for a friend’s message), 8 a day at most, and a chat sends at most one every 10 minutes (30 for a clan); nudges and cheers stay at 2 a day. Turn them off with the Friends and communities switch, which shows once you use them, mute a friend’s nudges or a chat’s messages, or turn off Toll’s notifications in the Settings app.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Toll sends anonymous usage counts to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service built for privacy, so we can see where Toll is hard to use: when Toll is opened; which first-run pages are reached and when the first run is finished; when the practice photo on the demo page is taken (with the habit's kind; it is a drawing, never a real photo); the range you pick for your daily phone time (such as "4 to 6 hours"); whether you ask for the free week's reminder and what iOS answered; each step of the Screen Time page (shown, continue, approved, declined, try again, and the type of error if one happens); how many apps you chose to lock, as a range (none, 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 or more), never which ones; how many days your first habit is due and the time of day you chose for its reminder; when the plans are shown and which plan you tap; when a purchase starts, completes, fails or is cancelled (with the plan and whether it has a free week), and when the renewal is turned off during the free week; when a proof is accepted (a photo, a stay at a habit's place or a step goal) or a photo is turned down (with the habit's kind and which check decided, and for an accepted proof, whether it came on the day you set up Toll); when a habit, schedule or time limit is made (with its kind); when a challenge's page is opened (with which challenge); and in Friends, clans and communities: when Friends or Communities is opened (and from where: the tab, Today, a notification or a link); when an invite is made and shared (and whether it carries a first name, never the name); when you accept an invite (by link or by code); how many friends you have after a pairing (1, 2, 3, 4 to 5, 6 to 10 or 11 to 20); when a nudge or a cheer is sent (with which cheer); when a friend’s page is opened (from the list, the ranking or a clan); when a friend’s nudges are muted or unmuted; when a friend is removed; whether you share your habits and where you answered (a question, the invite page or Settings); when a habit is hidden from friends or shown again; when a chat is opened and when a message is sent (with whether it is a chat with a friend, a clan or a community, never the message); when Toll refuses a message and the kind of reason, such as a banned word, a link, a contact detail or sending too fast; when someone reacts (with which of the four fixed reactions); when something is reported (a message or card, a person, a name or a clan name) or a person is blocked, and where; when you set or change your name in Toll; which rules you accept; whether your age range is 16 and over, under 16 or unknown; when a clan is started, joined (by link or by code) or left (and whether you started it), when a clan’s invite is shared and its ranking is opened; when a community is opened (from the tab, a notification or a link), joined or left, or its ranking opened (with which community); when your friends and communities data is deleted; and your answer to the notification question (allowed, not allowed or later). Each count carries an anonymous identifier (a scrambled form of the one iOS gives Toll on this iPhone) and technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, and settings such as language, region, time zone and text size. A count never carries your name, what you write, your photos, your apps, the websites you visit, where you are, your steps or your friends. TelemetryDeck does not store IP addresses. See telemetrydeck.com/privacy.

Toll shows no ads, does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not sell any data. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app, Apple may send us crash reports; they contain no photos and do not say who you are.

Toll's ads on Instagram and Facebook

Toll advertises on Instagram and Facebook. To see whether those ads work, Toll includes Meta's app SDK: when Toll is installed and opened, and when a free week or a purchase starts, it tells Meta so, with technical details such as the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the language and the time zone, and an anonymous identifier Meta's software creates on your iPhone. Toll never asks to track you, so Meta gets no advertising identifier and iOS blocks Meta's tracking address; whether an ad led to an install reaches Meta through Apple's SKAdNetwork, which does not say who you are. Meta never receives your name, what you write, your photos, your habits, your apps or where you are. See facebook.com/privacy/policy.

Your data, your choices

Apart from what Toll’s server keeps for friends, clans and communities, everything Toll keeps is on your devices and in your own iCloud, so you can see it, export it and delete it yourself. Export data in Toll’s Settings writes your habits, proof records, rest days and focus sessions to a file you keep. Deleting Toll deletes its data from the iPhone; Toll’s iCloud data is deleted from your iCloud storage in the Settings app. Delete my friends and communities data, in Friends, Communities or Settings, deletes what the server keeps. To have the purchase records RevenueCat holds deleted, write to us.

Children

Toll is for people managing their own phone. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. Chat in communities is for people 16 and over.

Toll's website

tollapp.github.io has no cookies, no analytics and no scripts, and loads nothing from other services. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visits, including IP addresses, to keep the service secure, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version is published in the app and on tollapp.github.io/privacy.

Privacy Policy

Toll keeps the apps you choose behind a gate until you prove today's habit with a photo. This page says what Toll does with your data. It is short because Toll collects almost nothing, and we never see what it keeps.

No account

Toll has no sign-up, no login and no server of its own. We cannot see who you are, and nothing you do in Toll reaches us.

Photos

Proof photos come from the live camera only. They are checked on your iPhone by Apple's on-device image analysis and on-device language model, then discarded. Toll never stores a photo, never uploads a photo, and never reads your photo library.

What Toll keeps

On your iPhone and in your private iCloud database: your habits (name, schedule, photos a day, kind, and for your own habit the description you wrote), proof records (the time of each photo, whether it was accepted and which check accepted it, never the photo), rest days and focus sessions (their times). They sync through your own iCloud account so they follow you to a new iPhone. With iCloud off, they stay on this iPhone.

On this iPhone only, and in its backups: your name, your onboarding answers (what matters to you, your age, what describes you and your daily phone time estimate), your settings, your game scores and the apps you put behind the gate. If you set a gate lock, only a salted fingerprint of the PIN is kept.

We have no access to any of it.

Screen Time

Toll uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks to shield the apps you pick. Apple does not tell Toll which apps those are; Toll works with tokens that stay on your iPhone. The Screen Time figures on the score page are drawn by iOS inside a Screen Time report that Toll cannot read, so the numbers never reach Toll and never leave your iPhone.

Toll never hides apps and never touches your passcode. If you turn on Hard lock, iOS also blocks deleting apps and keeps the date and time automatic while the gate is shut; both clear when it opens. Deleting Toll removes every shield.

Purchases

Toll Pro is sold through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment; Toll never sees your payment details. RevenueCat, the service that checks purchases for Toll, receives:

  • an anonymous identifier its software creates on your iPhone, and your Toll purchase and trial history;
  • with each request, technical details: the iPhone model, the iOS and Toll versions, the App Store country, your preferred languages and an identifier Apple gives Toll on this iPhone, plus your IP address, which every server sees.

This happens when Toll loads the plans or checks Toll Pro, from the first launch. RevenueCat never receives your name, email, photos or habits. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

Notifications

Toll sends two kinds of notification, both only with your permission: the evening reminder, off until you turn it on in Settings, and, after you start a free week, one reminder 2 or 3 days before it ends, as you chose. Nothing else. Both can be turned off in the Settings app.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

None. Toll has no analytics, no ads and no trackers, does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not sell or share any data. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app, Apple may send us crash reports; they contain no photos and do not say who you are.

Your data, your choices

Everything Toll keeps is on your devices and in your own iCloud, so you can see it, export it and delete it yourself. Export data in Toll's Settings writes your habits, proof records, rest days and focus sessions to a file you keep. Deleting Toll deletes its data from the iPhone; Toll's iCloud data is deleted from your iCloud storage in the Settings app. To have the purchase records RevenueCat holds deleted, write to us.

Children

Toll is for people managing their own phone. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

This website

This site has no cookies, no analytics and no scripts, and loads nothing from other services. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visits, including IP addresses, to keep the service secure, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version is published in the app and on this page.
